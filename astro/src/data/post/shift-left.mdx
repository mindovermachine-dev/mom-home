"Shift Left" is a term used in computer science, it is a bitwise operation: A shift left operation multiplies a binary number by 2; The decimal number `42` as 8-bit binary is `0010 1010` when you shift left you get `0101 0100` which is the decimal number `84`. This trick works for any decimal number, represented in binary.

The term "Shift Left" is used in the context of software development to indicate _a clever hack_, where you do an adorable simple trick in _one_ context to gain a huge impact in _another_. It's next level from "low hanging fruits" - it so effectful, that it's almost like magic or a trick.

In moderne age, the term "Shift Left" is adopted by quality workers as Quality Assurance term — Software Developers included — to indicate that the verification and validation process should "Shift Left". While it is still a reference to a smart hack, and the almost magical impact in quality you get from testing early rather than late. In this context it's also a reference to the general reading directions - things start at the left and progresses to the right.

So "Shift Left" has become a synonym with testing sooner in the process, closers to the source, more to the left; Building quality in — as opposed to glueing it on. It has become a mantra and a sign of recognition by a Continuous Delivery process and the Developer Experience (DevX) movement.

## Developer Experience (DevX)

A new term and movement grew out of the DevOps paradigm in the last decade or so; Developer Experience (DevX).

Inspired by the User Experience (UX) and Customer Experience (CX) disciplines focusing on answering the question:

> _— How does it fee to be a user of this product?_<br/>
> _— How does it feel to be a customer of this product?_

The DevX movement focuses on answering the question:

> _— How does it feel to be a developer of this product?_

DevX insists on a socio-technological agenda. It includes focus on the team leadership, the tool stack, the CI/CD pipeline, the development environment, the code review process, the overall culture of the development team. And advocates a meritocratic approach to recognizing and rewarding developers insights and contributions — as opposed to constantly being suppressed by short sighted commercial interests or ignorant manageres. It is about creating an environment where developers can thrive, be creative, and produce high-quality software.

In the DevOps and Continuous Delivery (CD) context, the annual State of DevOps reports from DORA (DevOps Research and Assessment) has — again and again — provided the insights, that optimizing DevX is the most impactful, and almost magical way, to improve software development processes — including quality, productivity and as a consequence hereof also revenue.

## Don't be evil

There is a widespread trend in digital online presence that we should all be aware of the US techbros' evil intentions. This worry seem to concentrate mostly on the negative impact of social medias and messaging platforms.

But this worry is also relevant to the software development stack.

However the concern is often overlooked by civilians (non-developers). But as AI is eating the world. A world that is already gulped by software, it turns out that the software development stack is potentially evil too.

- Cunning vendor lock-ins followed by spiralling costs.
- US based cloud providers that can not escape the dreaded US SCA and CLOUD acts, and consequently may be forced to allow US intelligence to secretly access data — even on non-US servers.
- Proprietary platforms, that sniffs your data and code, either for up selling or for AI training purposes.
- Proprietary protocols that you cannot inspect, extend or improve, and which you must pay for to use programmatically (APIs, SDKs)

While the evil social media platforms are well debated and discussed, the evilness of the software development stack is often overlooked. But this is really the true battle-field for independence, since our digital lives are rooted in software. It's an attack on the very foundation of our digital autonomy.

## Software is born free — and everywhere it is in chains

> _"Man is born free, and everywhere he is in chains."_

The quote is from Jean-Jacques Rousseau's book "The Social Contract" (1762). Rousseau's point being that humans are naturally independent and born without restrictions, but civilization places them under unjust rules, laws, and social controls.

The field of "Participatory Design" has been around for decades, rooted in Scandinavian traditions, and it is a field that advocates for the users of software to be involved in the design of the software they use. The claim is that software exist for the purpose of the users. It should serve humanity. Same basic pledge as academic research and knowledge. Participatory Designs speaks directly to the FOSS tradition.

FOSS=Free and Open Source Software.

Imagine a world without:

- Linux
- http/tcp
- HTML
- SMTP
- Git
- Apache Web Server
- PostgreSQL, MySQL
- Python, Rust, Go, PhP, C, C++, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java.
- React, Astro, Node.js

As some one who has spent all my professional life in software development, I often wonder if the world even knows the importance and impact that FOSS has on our lives.

Every single one of these technologies are Free and Open Source Software (FOSS). They are given to humanity at no cost. On terms similar to how academic knowledge is shared. The FOSS are stewarded by reliable not-for-profit software foundations that guarantee their continued development and maintenance and who guard their continued independence with the same fierce dedication as a lioness protects her newborn pups.

The list is endless, the impact of FOSS is everywhere. And taking away less than a handful of these technologies would make the entire world collapse. The internet would stop working, and the world would instantly be thrown back to the 1980s.

They are all part of a circular economy that decouples activity from growth and exploitation, and therefore they are nowhere present or visible in any GDP statistics. They are the silent invisible infrastructure of our digital lives.

Let me paraphrase Rousseau's quote in the context of software:

> _"Software is born free, and everywhere it is in chains."_

There is barely a single piece of software in the entire world that does not have a FOSS dependency. Facebook, Microsoft Office and any LLM that you can think of included.

But software is chained. Despite the importance of FOSS the vast majority of all software in the entire world is proprietary, and exists for the sole purpose of being _someone's_ mean to profit and exploitation.

## The rubric of evilness

The word _rubric_ originates from the Latin word _rubrica_, meaning "red ochre" or "red earth." In medieval times, it referred to the red ink used by scribes and monks to highlight liturgical directions, headings, or important rules in religious manuscripts. Over time, the term shifted from referring to the literal red text to the actual rules and guidelines themselves.

In academic and assessment contexts, a _rubric_ is a structured set of criteria for making judgments consistently.

The benfit of a rubric is that it provides a clear framework for evaluating complex concepts, ensuring that assessments are fair, transparent, and reproducible. It allows for nuanced evaluations by breaking down broad questions into specific criteria.

Yesterday, together with Claude Sonnet 🤖, I generated a rubric for evaluating the "evilness" of software tools, platforms, frameworks, protocols etc. This rubric is designed to help developers and organizations assess the ethical implications of the tools they use in their development stack. In short to measure the evilness of the tool.

The rubric uses _dimensions_ and _weights_ to quantify the evilness of a software tool. Each dimension represents a specific aspect of ethical concern, and the weights reflect the relative importance of each dimension in the overall assessment.

The dimensions include:

1. FOSS and source availability
2. Open standards and interoperability
3. Vendor lock-in and exit costs
4. User ownership and control
5. Privacy and surveillance
6. Business model and commercial incentives
7. Manipulation and attention capture
8. Governance and accountability
9. Communicative equality and power accountability (Would Habermas and Foucault be happy?)
10. Security, resilience, and decentralization
11. Labor, ecosystem, and social externalities

Some dimensions were added by Claude and some by me.

The rubric is designed as an AI skill, you can see it at [lakruzz/evils](https://github.com/lakruzz/evils), feel free to fork it at do with it what you will.

I generated a list of tools and platforms that was basically ...all over the place, but the clever thing about the rubric is really that it allows me to systematically evaluate each one based on the defined dimensions, despite the fact that they may have different characteristics.

My prompt was simply:

```text
/tool-evilness-assessment
Use the skill on the list in techstack.md
```

If you are curious, have a look at the [`digital-autonomy-assessment-full.md`](https://github.com/lakruzz/evils/blob/main/reports/digital-autonomy-assessment-full.md) **Note: The higher the score the more evil**

So in one end 😈 Meta scores 90 on both Facebook and Instagram, and in the other 😇 Forgejo, the selfhosted FOSS alternative to GitHub, scores 1.5.

## Under 30

Our initial plan is to settle on tools with an evil score under 30 - this is what we imagines:

- **VS Chromium** IDE; While the VS Code is open source, the Microsoft branded version is not. The Chromium version is fully FOSS and can be used without any Microsoft telemetry.
- **Forgejo** Git hosting; A self-hosted FOSS alternative to GitHub, allowing developers to maintain control over their code repositories, issues, projects releases etc.
- **Woodpecker CI** Continuous Integration; A self-hosted, open-source alternative to GitHub Actions, enabling developers to automate their build, test, and deployment processes.
- **Ollama, vLLm, LocalAI** Self-hosted LLM's on our infrastructure to connect to both from our IDEs and our pipeline runners; We will host and run LLM's on our own infrastructure, allowing developers to use them without relying on proprietary cloud providers or exposing their data to third parties.
- **Matrix** Team Communication; A decentralized, open standard for messaging and collaboration)
- **Element X, FluffyChat** (Populous Matrix clients; FOSS clients for the Matrix protocol, providing secure and private E2EE communication.
- **DevBox, DevEnv, Nix, Dagger.io, DevContainers, Docker, Podman, Kubernetes** Containerized Development Environment; A collection of FOSS tools for creating isolated and reproducible development environments that are detached from any proprietary cloud provider, allowing developers to maintain control over their development stack and run the exact same verification and validation in their local environments as the will later execute in the CI/CD pipeline.
- **Mastodon/ActivityPub** Social Media and community building; A decentralized, open-source social networking platform — The Fediverse — that allows users to create and share content without being subject to the control of a single corporation or platform.
- **POSSE** (Publish on your own Site, syndicate elsewhere); A strategy based on always only publishing content on your own website and then syndicating it to other platforms, giving you control over your content and audience. All documentation, blog posts, user manual, User voice channels, and other content will be published on your websites we host ourselves first, and only _then_ shared (syndicated) to social media platforms like Mastodon. With this approach, we still maintain the option to syndicate to other platforms with evil scores over 30.
